Let's clean up

One day. One city.
50 Lakhs to clean Bengaluru.

Teams across Bengaluru clean real public spaces in a single day. Waste is counted in standardised sacks. Proof is public and verifiable.

Prizes: ₹10 lakh, ₹5 lakh, ₹2.5 lakh — plus ₹1 lakh each for the next 20 teams.

₹10 lakh is kept aside so teams do not have to pay for basic safety and cleaning equipment themselves.

Pilot city: Bengaluru.

Why this

I was in Madurai recently and spent time around the temple area. It was filthy. This is a city that sees millions of visitors and has deep cultural and religious importance, yet the public spaces around it are treated like dumping grounds.

Recently, Madurai appeared on topped multiple lists of India’s dirtiest cities. That made the problem concrete. If this is the situation here, it makes sense to start here.

This is a pilot. One city. One day. A direct way to test whether incentives and public accountability can work.
