Let’s clean up

One day. One city.
₹50 lakh to clean Bengaluru.

Teams across Bengaluru clean real public spaces in a single day. Waste is counted in standardised sacks. Proof is public and verifiable.

Prizes: ₹10 lakh, ₹5 lakh, ₹2.5 lakh — plus ₹1 lakh each for the next 20 teams.

₹10 lakh is set aside so teams don’t pay for basic safety and cleaning equipment.

Pilot city: Bengaluru.

Why this

I was in Madurai recently and spent time around the temple area. It was filthy. This is a city with deep cultural and religious importance, yet public spaces around it are treated like dumping grounds.

Recently, Madurai appeared on topped multiple lists of India’s dirtiest cities. That made the problem concrete.

This is a pilot. One city. One day. A direct way to test whether incentives and public accountability can work.
